## Summary
A Duskfield woman, raised in a small village, visiting neighboring villages regularly for everyday goods along the established pathways. As a child she found the silence around her oppressive in a way she couldn't articulate — her ears would grow sore from quiet. She didn't understand why her parents never relocated to another village like others sometimes did.

**Age 4:** Her brother is born. She describes him as the most wonderful creature she had ever seen.

**Age 9:** [Unresolved — the inciting incident.] Something happens, involving her and her 5-year-old brother, with consequences worse than she anticipated. The event costs her brother's life. It is — and this is critical — _preventable_, in a way that hinges on the absence of recorded knowledge. Something that could have been written down, warned about, or known, and wasn't, because Duskfield doesn't record things.

**Age 9–10:** She doesn't process this as grief at first. Duskfield culture has no vocabulary for loss — acknowledging it would require speech, and speech is what they don't do. It takes roughly a year for her to recognize she is going through something — a "phase" she can't name, doesn't know how to describe, and doesn't know if it will end. She wants things to be different. She doesn't yet know what that means.

**Age 13:** A child in a nearby Duskfield village dies of the _same preventable cause_. This is the snap. Everything she's swallowed for four years surfaces at once. She becomes angry — not abstractly, but specifically: there's no record of her brother's death, nothing was learned, nothing was prevented, and a second child paid for that same absence. She begins to understand that Ashkeep records things — and wonders, with real anguish, whether something she could have done after her brother's death might have saved this second child.

**Age 13–15:** [Unresolved — two formative years.] She sits with this. Possibly tests the edges of Duskfield's silence in small ways. Her relationship with her parents during this period is unresolved — does she voice any of this to them? Do they shut it down gently, the way Duskfield handles everything? Or does one of them, once, almost not manage to hide that they understand exactly what she's feeling — suggesting they once had the same questions and buried them?

**Age 15+:** She leaves Duskfield to travel between factions — the mechanism for this journey is still undetermined, and is explicitly a plot question, not just a character one.
