**fragment_001**  
_topic: duskfield_morning | tier: 0 | tags: ambient, sensory_

The light comes in sideways through woven walls. Pale. The kind of light that doesn't announce itself. She has been awake for some time already, lying still the way Duskfield people lie still — not resting, just waiting for the body to agree that the day has begun. Outside, something small moves across the roof. A bird, maybe. She doesn't look up.

**fragment_002**  
_topic: silence_rule | tier: 1 | tags: duskfield_core, silence_

In Duskfield the silence is not the absence of sound. There is wind. There is the knock of wooden tools. There is singing — always singing, more than anywhere else she has since been. What is absent is the word. The specific, directed, meaning-carrying word aimed at another person. Children learn this the way they learn not to touch fire. Not because someone explains it. Because the air itself seems to thicken around the attempt.

**fragment_003**  
_topic: grief_unnamed | tier: 1 | tags: protagonist, grief, second_generation_

She does not have a word for what this year has been. In Duskfield, you don't. You have the tightness below the sternum. You have the way certain hours of the afternoon become impossible to be inside of. You have the thing her hands do sometimes, reaching for something that isn't there, then stopping, then pretending they were doing something else all along.

**fragment_004**  
_topic: mother_npc | tier: 1 | tags: parent, voluntary_keeper, trauma_coping_

Her mother was not born here. This matters, though she couldn't have said why until much later. Her mother chose this — the village, the quiet, the life built at the edges of things. She brought that choice into every ordinary motion: the way she sorted dried herbs by feel rather than sight, the way she would pause in the middle of a task and simply stand, looking at nothing, before continuing. As if checking something inside herself. As if making sure it was still there.

**fragment_005**  
_topic: mother_npc | tier: 0 | tags: parent, opening_scene, ambient_

Morning. Her mother is outside, sitting with her back to the hut wall, doing the thing with the dried stems — separating them by texture, bundling them, setting them aside. She does not look up when the door opens. She shifts slightly, just enough to make room on the ground beside her. That is all. That is everything.

**fragment_006**  
_topic: father_npc | tier: 1 | tags: parent, threshold_memory, displacement_

Her father makes things. Has always made things. Small wooden objects, mostly — jointed, fitted, no obvious purpose. He gives them away almost immediately, as if keeping them would cost him something. She used to think this was generosity. She has since wondered if it is the opposite: if the making is the point, and the giving-away is how you make sure you never have to look at what you made for too long.

**fragment_007**  
_topic: brother | tier: 2 | tags: inciting_incident, grief, absence_

He would have been six. She catches herself calculating this sometimes, the age he would have been, as if somewhere a version of him is still growing. The calculation always lands wrong. Six is too specific. Too real. She stops before she gets there.

**fragment_008**  
_topic: silence_rule | tier: 2 | tags: duskfield_core, containment, protagonist_arc_

The silence rule was never explained to her. This is the thing she keeps returning to. Not that it exists — she understood that early. But that no one ever said: here is why. Here is what it protects. Here is the cost of breaking it. It was simply there, the way gravity is there, and she moved around it for fifteen years before she thought to ask what would happen if she didn't.

**fragment_009**  
_topic: duskfield_village | tier: 0 | tags: ambient, world_

The village is small enough that you can walk its full perimeter in the time it takes the light to change. The paths between huts are worn smooth — not by design, just by years of the same feet taking the same routes. Some paths go nowhere useful now. Someone used to live at the end of them.

---

**fragment_010**  
_topic: kindness_rule | tier: 1 | tags: duskfield_core, faction_mechanic_

No one in Duskfield turns a stranger away. This is not hospitality exactly — it is closer to policy. A stranger arrives, space is made, food is shared, no questions are asked about where they came from or why. This has always seemed to her like kindness. She is only now beginning to wonder if it is something else: if the ease with which Duskfield absorbs exiles is itself a kind of system. If the welcome is the cage.
